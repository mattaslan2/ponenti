## Common US payment terms

Payment terms tell the buyer when an invoice is due. Common US B2B terms include:

- **Net 30:** full payment is due 30 days after the invoice date.
- **Net 60:** the same rule, at 60 days.
- **2/10 net 30:** the buyer takes 2% off if it pays within 10 days. Otherwise the full amount is due on day 30.

Early-payment discounts cost you more than they look. The buyer earns 2% for paying 20 days early. Annualized, that is roughly 37% (2/98 x 365/20). Offer the discount when you need the cash, not out of habit.

Large retailers may set terms through their own vendor agreements. Put your terms in the contract and on every invoice.

## Retailer deductions and chargebacks

Retailers can short-pay an invoice by taking deductions for shortages, damage, pricing differences or promotional allowances. Some also fine suppliers for breaking their vendor rules, such as wrong labels, late shipments or missing advance ship notices. These fines are called chargebacks.

Track every deduction from the remittance advice. Dispute the ones you think are wrong, with documents, inside the window set in your vendor agreement. Miss the window and you may lose the right to dispute.

## A day-by-day dunning schedule

Dunning is the sequence of reminders and follow-ups on an unpaid invoice. The schedule below is an example, not a rule.

| Day | Step |
| --- | --- |
| 5 days before due | Short reminder email with a copy of the invoice |
| Due date | Email asking for payment status |
| Due + 3-7 days | Call the buyer's accounts payable team |
| Due + 15 days | Second call, get a payment date in writing |
| Due + 30 days | Formal letter, decide on a credit hold for new orders |
| Due + 60 days | Final written notice, payment plan if needed |
| Due + 90 days | Hand off to a licensed collection agency or an attorney |

## Why US hours and your own name matter

US accounts payable teams work US hours. While US daylight saving time is in effect (March to November), New York is 7 hours behind Türkiye. The rest of the year the gap is 8 hours. A 9:00-17:00 New York workday runs 16:00-24:00 Türkiye time in summer. The West Coast is 3 hours further behind.

A call gives you a chance to fix a problem in one conversation. Late payment sometimes has an administrative cause. Examples: an invoice never entered, a missing purchase order (PO) number, a missing W-9 form or a wrong remit-to address. A call can surface these quickly.

When the caller speaks in your company's name, the call stays a supplier conversation. That makes the relationship easier to protect. When a third-party agency calls, the buyer may read it as the account changing hands. That is why your own team, or a desk working in your name, can handle follow-up for the first 90 days. This is a practice choice, not a guaranteed result. Whoever follows up, payment should always go straight to your bank account.

## The legal frame: FDCPA and state licensing

The federal Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA) covers consumer debts. The FTC says it plainly: "Business debts are not covered by the FDCPA" ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/debt-collection-faqs)). The statute defines debt by reference to personal, family or household purposes ([FDCPA text](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/fair-debt-collection-practices-act-text)).

States can add their own rules. California, for example, extended its consumer debt collection requirements to certain small business debts. The change applies to such debts entered into, sold or assigned on or after July 1, 2025 ([DFPI](https://dfpi.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/highlights-of-2024-chaptered-legislation.pdf)).

Collection agency licensing is also set state by state. Washington requires a license for a business that collects debts on behalf of a client ([Washington](https://dol.wa.gov/professional-licenses/collection-agencies/get-your-license-collection-agencies)). Florida requires agencies that collect commercial claims owed to others to register ([Florida](https://flofr.gov/divisions-offices/division-of-consumer-finance/commercial-collection-agencies)). If an outside team follows up in your name, check the rules in the debtor's state.

Accounts more than 90 days late are usually handed to a licensed collection agency.

## How much cash faster payment frees

The formula: annual US sales / 365 x (current payment days - target days).

Current payment days is how long your customers take to pay, on average. It is often called DSO (days sales outstanding). A quick estimate: accounts receivable balance / annual sales x 365.

Example: annual US sales of $3,000,000, a current DSO of 62 days and a target of 40 days.

- $3,000,000 / 365 = about $8,219 of sales per day
- 62 - 40 = 22 days
- $8,219 x 22 = about $181,000

That is the cash tied up in the gap, worked out as arithmetic. It is not a promise. The real result depends on your customers and your contracts.

## What to do now

1. Calculate your current DSO: accounts receivable / annual US sales x 365.
2. Put payment terms in every contract and on every invoice. Offer early-payment discounts on purpose.
3. Set up a follow-up schedule like the one above, and start before the due date.
4. Review retailer deductions weekly and log every dispute deadline.
5. For accounts past 90 days, pick a collection agency. If the debtor's state requires a license or registration, confirm the agency has it.
