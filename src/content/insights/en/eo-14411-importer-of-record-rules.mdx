## What happened, and when

Executive Order 14411, "Strengthening Customs Enforcement," was signed June 3, 2026 and published June 10 in the [Federal Register (91 FR 35125)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/10/2026-11595/strengthening-customs-enforcement).

It targets the importer of record (IOR), the party liable for customs duties. US Customs and Border Protection (CBP) is deactivating unused numbers and can void inaccurate ones.

| Date | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| July 16, 2026 | "Inactive for Entry Purposes" status goes live for numbers with no entry in 366 days ([CBP CSMS #69241265](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/42089b1)). |
| September 18, 2026 | Voiding begins for numbers with inaccurate or incomplete Form 5106 data, per CBP's August 19 notice ([91 FR 53627](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/19/2026-16911/accuracy-of-importer-of-record-data-submitted-to-cbp)). |
| November 30, 2026 | The order's 180-day window closes, counted from June 3. |

As of October 5, 2026, the [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/agencies/u-s-customs-and-border-protection) shows no final rule for the order's other measures.

## Which addresses Form 5106 no longer accepts

IOR numbers come from CBP Form 5106 ([19 CFR 24.5](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-19/section-24.5)), which asks for the importer's name, tax ID (EIN) or CBP-assigned number, addresses, phone and email ([CBP notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/19/2026-16911/accuracy-of-importer-of-record-data-submitted-to-cbp)).

[CBP's notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/19/2026-16911/accuracy-of-importer-of-record-data-submitted-to-cbp) says the physical address must be the business's actual location. It cannot be:

- a registered agent address
- a customs broker or freight forwarder address
- a P.O. box
- a business service center
- the address of another person or company

An owner's home address can qualify if tied to the business. Email and phone must be the importer's own, not a broker's or third party's.

CBP is reviewing records on file too: if data is inaccurate or incomplete, it voids the number, making it invalid for any purpose, including entry. CBP emails the importer and, if applicable, the last filing broker.

Reinstatement requests go to IORProgram@cbp.dhs.gov, subject line "Enforcing IOR Accuracy." Intentional false statements on the form can bring fines or prison under 18 U.S.C. 1001.

## Unused numbers are being deactivated

Under [19 CFR 24.5(e)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-19/section-24.5), numbers unused for one year with no open transactions are removed from CBP files. Since July 16, 2026, CBP can automatically deactivate numbers after 366 days without an entry ([CBP CSMS #69241265](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/42089b1)).

Inactive numbers cannot file entries; non-entry functions such as bonds still work. Your broker can reactivate one by resubmitting full Form 5106 data ([CBP CSMS #69056621](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/41db86d)).

## What EO 14411 requires

The order gives the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) [180 days](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/10/2026-11595/strengthening-customs-enforcement) to act on:

- minimum US assets, bonding or both for every IOR, with higher minimum bonds
- a named IOR and a bond or assets for all formal and informal entries
- disclosures of expected import volumes, year organized, ownership, beneficial owners, affiliates and US assets
- "good standing" based on compliance and payment history; IORs without it cannot import
- a registry update: inactive IORs removed, risk tiers added
- recurrent vetting of importers, brokers and freight forwarders

It closes November 30, 2026: a deadline for DHS action, not an effective date.

## Extra requirements for foreign importers

For foreign IORs, the order requires action ["promptly,"](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/10/2026-11595/strengthening-customs-enforcement) with no fixed deadline:

- no informal entry, the simplified process generally used for shipments up to $2,500 ([19 CFR 143.21](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-19/section-143.21))
- no continuous bond for formal entries unless CBP grants an exception
- CTPAT validation, or entries filed by a CTPAT-validated customs broker

Under [Section 10 of the order](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/10/2026-11595/strengthening-customs-enforcement), a company is a "U.S. IOR" only if it is organized and located in the US and always controlled by beneficial owners who are US citizens or green card holders. Owning significant US real property, as DHS determines, also qualifies. Every other IOR is a "foreign IOR."

"Located in the United States" means at least a US principal place of business, significant business activity at a US site, and sufficient US assets.

Read literally, a US company controlled by Turkish owners without US citizenship or green cards is a foreign IOR unless it owns significant US real property. DHS will set the details.

## Your broker's role, and Ponenti's

"Customs business" covers entries, classification, valuation, duty payments and refunds, and preparing documents for filing with CBP ([19 CFR 111.1](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-19/section-111.1)). Doing it for others requires a customs broker license, with narrow exceptions. Importers may handle their own ([19 CFR 111.2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-19/section-111.2)).

Your broker must sign the power of attorney directly with your company, not through a forwarder or other third party ([19 CFR 111.36(c)(3)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-19/section-111.36)). [CBP's notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/19/2026-16911/accuracy-of-importer-of-record-data-submitted-to-cbp) also tells brokers to exercise due diligence and not to transmit unverified information.

Ponenti's role is limited. It never acts as importer of record and never prepares or files customs entries or CBP refund requests; your licensed customs broker files them. Ponenti never holds or moves client money. It can audit your importer record and help you gather the owner and address documents your broker asks for.

## What to do now

1. Ask your licensed customs broker for the Form 5106 data CBP holds. Confirm the name, EIN, addresses, email and phone are yours.
2. If the physical address is a registered agent, P.O. box, business service center or broker address, have your broker replace it with your actual location.
3. Confirm your power of attorney was signed directly with your broker.
4. No entry in 12 months? Have your number's status checked and reactivated before your next shipment.
5. Gather ownership, beneficial owner and US asset records now. Watch DHS announcements through November 30, 2026.
