## What happened, and when

On July 23, 2026, the Office of the United States Trade Representative (USTR) closed its Section 301 investigations into 60 economies and imposed tariffs on their goods for not banning imports made with forced labor. For Türkiye the rate is 12.5%, "except as provided in Annex I and Annex II, Part A" of the notice ([91 FR 47318](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/28/2026-15181/notice-of-actions-in-section-301-investigations-of-acts-policies-and-practices-of-various-economies)).

The duty is collected under HTSUS heading 9903.05.79 and applies to goods entered for consumption, or withdrawn from a warehouse for consumption, on or after 12:01 a.m. Eastern time on July 24, 2026 ([CBP CSMS #69326983](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/421d887)).

| Date | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| February 24, 2026 | IEEPA duties stop being collected; a 10% Section 122 surcharge starts the same day, for 150 days ([Proclamation 11012](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-02-25/html/2026-03824.htm)). See [IEEPA refunds](/en/insights/ieepa-refunds-us-bank-details). |
| March 12, 2026 | USTR opens Section 301 investigations into 60 economies, Türkiye among them (initiation notice at 91 FR 12884). |
| June 5, 2026 | Proposed action published (91 FR 34272); comments and hearings follow. |
| July 24, 2026 | The Section 122 surcharge expires at 12:01 a.m.; the Section 301 duty takes effect at the same minute. |
| July 28, 2026 | The in-transit window closes at 12:01 a.m.; the final notice is published in the Federal Register. |

## Why 12.5% and not 10%

USTR set two rates. Economies that have a forced labor import ban, that committed to one in an Agreement on Reciprocal Trade, or that run a partial regime blocking certain forced labor goods pay 10%. Every other investigated economy pays 12.5%. Türkiye is in the second group ([91 FR 47318](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/28/2026-15181/notice-of-actions-in-section-301-investigations-of-acts-policies-and-practices-of-various-economies)).

The 12.5% is an additional ad valorem duty: it is added to the normal (most-favored-nation) duty of the product, and goods stay subject to antidumping, countervailing and other duties, taxes and fees ([CBP CSMS #69326983](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/421d887)). It is not a replacement rate.

## What is exempt

CBP's guidance lists the exemption headings. A product of Türkiye does not pay the 12.5% if it falls under one of them ([CBP CSMS #69326983](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/421d887)):

- 9903.05.85: goods loaded on a vessel and in transit on their final mode before 12:01 a.m. Eastern time on July 24, 2026, and entered before 12:01 a.m. on July 28, 2026
- 9903.05.86 and 9903.05.87: the products listed in U.S. notes 52(b) and 52(c) to subchapter III of chapter 99 (the raw materials, energy and agricultural lists in the notice's annexes)
- 9903.05.88: civil aircraft, engines, parts and components, and ground flight simulators
- 9903.05.89: pharmaceutical products
- 9903.05.90: aluminum, steel, copper and their derivative articles; passenger vehicles, light trucks and their parts; medium- and heavy-duty vehicles and parts; wood products; semiconductors
- 9903.05.91 and 9903.05.92: donations to relieve human suffering, and informational materials

USTR's fact sheet puts the last group more simply: "all articles and parts of articles subject to Section 232 tariffs" are exempt ([USTR fact sheet](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/fact-sheets/2026/july/fact-sheet-ustr-section-301-action-response-failure-60-economies-ban-imports-produced-forced-labor)). Those goods pay their Section 232 duty instead.

Two more rules from the same guidance: goods entered under a chapter 98 provision are generally not subject to the duty, except that goods returned after repair, alteration, processing or assembly abroad (9802.00.40 to 9802.00.80) pay it on the foreign value added; and goods admitted to a foreign trade zone on or after July 24 must take privileged foreign status, so the duty follows them out of the zone.

The exemption lists are long and specific. Whether your product is on one is a classification question for your licensed customs broker, line by line, not a guess from the product name.

## What it does to a landed price

Take $100,000 of goods at customs value with a normal duty rate of 3%, as an example.

| Line | Before July 24, 2026 | Since July 24, 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal duty, 3% | $3,000 | $3,000 |
| Section 122 surcharge, 10% | $10,000 | 0 |
| Section 301 duty, 12.5% | 0 | $12,500 |
| Duty, total | $13,000 | $15,500 |

The merchandise processing fee and the harbor maintenance fee come on top, as before. Against the first weeks of 2026, when IEEPA duties were still collected, the comparison depends on the product's IEEPA rate; against 2024, when none of these duties existed, the same shipment now pays about five times the duty.

The Census Bureau's import data shows the same thing for all goods from Türkiye together. The effective duty rate, calculated duty divided by the value of imports for consumption, was 3.3% in the twelve months to August 2024, 6.9% in the twelve months to August 2025 and 15.3% in the twelve months to August 2026. Month by month in 2026: 17.9% in January, 17.3% in February, between 12.0% and 13.5% from March to July, and 14.6% in August, the first full month of the Section 301 duty ([Census International Trade API](https://www.census.gov/data/developers/data-sets/international-trade.html), data through August 2026). The [trade data page](/en/trade-data) has the live series by product.

## Who pays it

The importer of record pays the duty at entry, or on its broker's periodic statement, weeks before the US customer pays the invoice. Who ends up bearing it is a matter of the sales terms:

- DDP: the seller's side pays. If you sell through your own US company, that company is the importer of record and the duty is its cost.
- DAP, FOB and similar terms: the US buyer's company is the importer of record and pays the duty. Expect requests to share it.

Price lists, pro forma invoices and contracts dated before July 24, 2026 were built on a 10% surcharge or none. Each one needs a new landed-cost line, and the open orders need a decision on who absorbs the difference.

## Ponenti's role is limited

Ponenti never acts as importer of record and never prepares or files customs entries; your licensed customs broker classifies each line and files it. Ponenti never holds or moves client money. It can rebuild your landed-cost model and price list with the new duty, keep the in-transit evidence with your entry records, and work the collection of invoices that now carry it.

## What to do now

1. Ask your broker which of your HTS lines fall under 9903.05.79 and which under an exemption heading, in writing.
2. For entries between July 24 and 28, 2026, keep the bill of lading showing the loading date and the entry summary showing 9903.05.85.
3. Rebuild the landed-cost model: customs value, normal duty, 12.5%, fees, freight, insurance. Reissue price lists and pro forma invoices dated before July 24.
4. Decide, order by order, who absorbs the duty on goods already sold, and confirm it in writing with the customer.
5. Check your product's effective duty rate on the [trade data page](/en/trade-data) each month; the Census Bureau publishes about five weeks after month end.
