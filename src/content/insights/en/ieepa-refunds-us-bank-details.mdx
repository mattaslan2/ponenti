## What happened

On February 20, 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court ruled on tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA). The cases were Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump and Trump v. V.O.S. Selections, Inc. The holding fits in one line: ["IEEPA does not authorize the President to impose tariffs."](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/24-1287_4gcj.pdf)

The same day, [Executive Order 14389](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/02/ending-certain-tariff-actions/) ended those duties. U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) [stopped collecting IEEPA duties](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/40b11c9) on goods entered on or after February 24, 2026.

CBP estimates that IEEPA duties assessed from February 3, 2025 through February 24, 2026 total about $166 billion. More than 53 million entry summaries need processing to issue the refunds ([91 FR 63300](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/05/2026-20228/agency-information-collection-activities-court-ordered-refunds-under-the-international-emergency)).

## How refunds are claimed

CBP built a refund tool called CAPE inside ACE, its online system of record for imports. CAPE [went live on April 20, 2026](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/413a632) and its scope [was expanded on June 29, 2026](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/41de055).

Only two parties can file a CAPE Declaration: the importer of record (IOR) itself, or the customs broker that filed the entries. CBP set this out in [CSMS # 68340863](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/412cc7f). CSMS messages are CBP's official trade notices.

For standard unliquidated entries, valid refunds [generally issue within 60-90 days](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/412cc7f) after a CAPE Declaration is accepted. Further review can take longer. Refunds are [consolidated into one payment](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/412cc7f) to the IOR or to the third party it names on CBP Form 4811.

## Where refunds stand in October 2026

In a sworn declaration filed with the Court of International Trade on September 15, 2026, CBP reported two figures as of September 11 ([CBP declaration, Freestyle World v. CBP, No. 26-01088](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cit.19175/gov.uscourts.cit.19175.50.0.pdf)):

- about $122 billion in refunds, duties plus interest, completed in CAPE and sent to the Treasury for payment
- 20,184 refunds, about $1.3 billion, not sent because the importer of record had given no ACH bank details

CAPE Phase 3 opened on October 6, 2026. It covers finally liquidated entries filed by plaintiffs, for which the court has ordered reliquidation ([same declaration](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cit.19175/gov.uscourts.cit.19175.50.0.pdf)). An importer that has not sued is not in Phase 3.

On October 8, 2026, the court certified a class in the same case: all importers of record that paid IEEPA duties between February 1, 2025 and February 19, 2026, except those that filed their own action ([Freestyle World v. CBP, Court No. 26-01088](https://www.cit.uscourts.gov/sites/cit/files/26-123.pdf)). Membership is automatic, with no form to join or leave, and the court expects refunds with interest through liquidation or reliquidation. The order can still be appealed.

## Why your U.S. bank details decide the outcome

[Since February 6, 2026, CBP has issued all refunds electronically](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/02/2025-24171/electronic-refunds). A paper check requires an [approved waiver](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/02/2025-24171/electronic-refunds), and the waiver grounds in [31 CFR 208.4](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-A/part-208/section-208.4) are narrow.

Payment goes by ACH (Automated Clearing House), the U.S. electronic bank transfer network. CBP's ACH refund application [requires a U.S. bank account](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/02/2025-24171/electronic-refunds).

An importer without a U.S. account has two options. It can open one, or it can use CBP Form 4811 to name a refund recipient that has one. CBP's rule says [foreign importers will need a U.S. bank account or must authorize their broker](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/02/2025-24171/electronic-refunds) to receive refunds for them.

If bank details are missing, an approved refund [is rejected](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/02/2025-24171/electronic-refunds). In a [Federal Register notice dated October 5, 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/05/2026-20228/agency-information-collection-activities-court-ordered-refunds-under-the-international-emergency), CBP said it is holding refunds for filers with no ACH account on file in ACE. It will do the same for IEEPA refunds.

Waiting does not earn extra interest either. If CBP approves a refund within 30 days of liquidation but cannot send it solely because bank details are missing, [no delinquency interest accrues](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-19/chapter-I/part-24/section-24.36).

As of March 27, 2026, CBP reported more than 12,300 approved refunds rejected since February 6 for missing banking information ([CSMS # 68179006](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/410543e)). That count covers all CBP refunds and predates IEEPA refund payments, which CBP said would start [as early as May 12, 2026](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/415c8e9).

## Where refunds get stuck

| Situation | Result |
|---|---|
| Your company abroad is the IOR and has no U.S. account | The refund waits. You need a U.S. account or a Form 4811 designee ([91 FR 23](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/02/2025-24171/electronic-refunds)). |
| No ACE Portal account | Bank details cannot be entered. Open an account first ([91 FR 22](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/02/2025-24171/electronic-refunds)). |
| No email on your CBP Form 5106 (importer identity record) | The ACE account cannot be created automatically ([91 FR 27](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/02/2025-24171/electronic-refunds)). The email must belong to the IOR. Since September 18, 2026, inaccurate 5106 data can get the IOR number voided ([91 FR 53627](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/19/2026-16911/accuracy-of-importer-of-record-data-submitted-to-cbp)). |
| Your Form 4811 designee is not enrolled in ACH | The refund defaults to your ACH account. With no account, it is rejected ([91 FR 23](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/02/2025-24171/electronic-refunds)). |

## Can a rejected refund be reissued?

Yes. First, complete the ACH refund application in the ACE Portal. Then email CBP at frn-achrefundsupport@cbp.dhs.gov and ask that the rejected refunds be sent to the new account ([91 FR 23](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/02/2025-24171/electronic-refunds)).

Rejected refunds appear in the REV-613 report in ACE Reports. On August 18, 2026, CBP urged brokers to run this report for their importers. Importers can also run it themselves ([CSMS # 69570464](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/4258fa0)).

## Section 122 is a separate question

From February 24 to July 24, 2026, most imports, including goods from Türkiye, paid a 10% surcharge under Section 122 of the Trade Act ([Proclamation 11012](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-02-25/html/2026-03824.htm)). On May 7, 2026, the Court of International Trade held the proclamation invalid ([Slip Op. 26-47](https://www.cit.uscourts.gov/sites/cit/files/26-47.pdf)). The injunction and the refunds covered only the importer plaintiffs: the State of Washington, Burlap and Barrel and Basic Fun ([Slip Op. 26-53](https://www.cit.uscourts.gov/sites/cit/files/26-53.pdf)). The government appealed.

Other importers are not owed Section 122 refunds under that judgment. Recovering them takes a lawsuit of your own, and claims under the court's residual jurisdiction must be filed within two years after they accrue ([28 U.S.C. 2636(i)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/2636)). That is a question for a trade attorney.

## What Ponenti does and does not do

Preparing a refund request for CBP is "customs business." The definition in [19 CFR 111.1](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-19/chapter-I/part-111/subpart-A/section-111.1) covers refunds and the preparation of documents to be filed with CBP. Doing that work for someone else [requires a CBP broker license](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-19/chapter-I/part-111/subpart-A/section-111.2). An importer may do it [for its own account](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-19/chapter-I/part-111/subpart-A/section-111.2).

Ponenti never acts as importer of record. Ponenti does not prepare or file customs entries or CBP refund requests. You or your licensed customs broker file them.

Ponenti never holds or moves client money, so Ponenti cannot be your Form 4811 refund recipient.

What Ponenti can do: spot a missing refund setup, flag it, and tell you what to ask your broker and your bank.

## What to do now

1. Ask your broker to run the REV-613 report to see whether any refunds were rejected.
2. In your ACE Portal account, confirm that U.S. bank details appear under the ACH Refund Authorization tab.
3. Confirm with your broker that the email, phone number, and address on your Form 5106 are your own and current.
4. If you have no U.S. account, ask your bank about U.S. account options. If you use Form 4811 instead, agree in writing with your broker on when, and at what cost, the money reaches you.
5. If any refunds were rejected, enroll first, then email frn-achrefundsupport@cbp.dhs.gov to request reissue.
6. If your entries are finally liquidated and you have not sued, ask a trade attorney now whether to file. The class order can still be appealed, and court deadlines keep running.
