## What Form 5472 is

Form 5472 is an information return filed with the IRS (the US federal tax agency). It reports transactions between a US company and its related parties, such as a foreign owner or an affiliate abroad. It calculates no tax, but skipping it is expensive.

Under the [IRS instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472), a "reporting corporation" files Form 5472 for any year in which it had a reportable transaction with a related party. The rule comes from section 6038A of the Internal Revenue Code (IRC). A reporting corporation is either:

- **A 25% foreign-owned US corporation:** at any time during the tax year, one foreign person owns at least 25% of the vote or value, directly or indirectly ([IRC 6038A(c)(1)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleF-chap61-subchapA-partIII-subpartA-sec6038A.htm)).
- **A foreign corporation engaged in a US trade or business.**

## Single-member LLCs are covered too

For income tax, a single-member LLC (limited liability company) is usually a "disregarded entity": the IRS does not treat it as separate from its owner. Form 5472 is different. [Treasury regulations published in December 2016](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2016/12/13/2016-29641/treatment-of-certain-domestic-entities-disregarded-as-separate-from-their-owners-as-corporations-for) treat a US disregarded entity wholly owned by one foreign person as a corporation for this purpose. The [instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) apply this to tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2017.

For these LLCs, reportable transactions include payments tied to forming, acquiring or dissolving the company. That covers "contributions to, and distributions from, the entity" ([Part V instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472)). An LLC with no sales can still be required to file.

## How and when to file

- **US corporations** attach Form 5472 to Form 1120, the US corporate income tax return.
- **Foreign-owned single-member LLCs** attach it to a "pro forma" Form 1120, completing only the name, address and two items on page 1. They cannot e-file Form 5472 and must fax or mail it to a dedicated IRS address ([instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472)).
- **One form per related party.** Each related party with reportable transactions needs its own Form 5472.

The deadline is the Form 1120 due date, including extensions: generally the 15th day of the 4th month after the tax year ends ([Form 1120 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120)). For a calendar year, that is April 15. Filing [Form 7004](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i7004) by then generally extends it 6 months, to October 15. A foreign-owned LLC follows its owner's US tax year, if any; otherwise the calendar year.

## The penalty: $25,000 per form, per year

Under [IRC 6038A(d)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleF-chap61-subchapA-partIII-subpartA-sec6038A.htm), failing to file Form 5472 on time, or failing to keep the required records, costs $25,000 for each tax year. The [regulations](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-1/subject-group-ECFR31749dec6d4756f/section-1.6038A-4) apply this per related party, and a "substantially incomplete" form counts as not filed.

If the failure continues more than 90 days after the IRS mails a notice, another $25,000 applies. It repeats for each 30-day period, or part of one. The [IRS penalty page](https://www.irs.gov/payments/international-information-reporting-penalties) is direct: "There is no maximum penalty amount."

## Worked example

This is arithmetic on the statutory amount, not a prediction of what the IRS will assess. It assumes one related party and no continuation penalty.

| Missed years | Statutory amount |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | $25,000 |
| 2 | $50,000 |
| 3 | $75,000 |

Three missed years equal $75,000 before any continuation penalty. Each additional related party can add $25,000 per year. In the [regulation's own example](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-1/subject-group-ECFR31749dec6d4756f/section-1.6038A-4), the forms arrive 135 days after the IRS notice. The 45 days past the 90-day period count as two periods, one full and one partial: $75,000 per year, $225,000 for three years.

## The assessment window stays open

The IRS normally has 3 years after a return is filed to assess tax ([IRC 6501(a)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleF-chap66-subchapA-sec6501.htm)). [IRC 6501(c)(8)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleF-chap66-subchapA-sec6501.htm) changes this when Form 5472 information is missing. The period "shall not expire before the date which is 3 years after" the IRS receives it. If the form is never filed, the window never closes. With reasonable cause, the extension covers only the related items.

## Relief for reasonable cause

The statute allows relief for reasonable cause ([IRC 6038A(d)(3)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleF-chap61-subchapA-partIII-subpartA-sec6038A.htm)). The [regulations](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-1/subject-group-ECFR31749dec6d4756f/section-1.6038A-4) require a written statement of the facts, signed under penalties of perjury. They also tell the IRS to apply it "liberally" to small corporations, meaning gross receipts of $20 million or less. The company must not have known the rule, must have limited US presence, and must cooperate promptly and fully with IRS requests.

Late filing alone does not cancel the penalty. For companies not under examination and not yet contacted by the IRS, the [IRS procedure](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/delinquent-international-information-return-submission-procedures) is to file through normal channels. A reasonable cause statement can be attached, but "penalties may be assessed without considering" it at first.

## What to do now

1. **Check every tax year since formation.** Confirm Form 5472 was filed each year, and get copies plus proof of fax or mailing.
2. **Map your related parties.** List each foreign owner, parent or affiliate that dealt with the US company. Each needs its own form ([instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472)).
3. **Gather transaction records by year.** Invoices, loan agreements, intercompany payments and bank records of capital in and out. The law requires keeping them anyway ([IRC 6038A(a)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleF-chap61-subchapA-partIII-subpartA-sec6038A.htm)).
4. **Calendar the next deadline.** For a calendar-year company: April 15, or October 15 if Form 7004 is filed by April 15.
5. **Ask a qualified tax professional about reasonable cause before filing late forms,** so the statement of facts goes in with the filing.
