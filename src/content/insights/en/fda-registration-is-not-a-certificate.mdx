## The short answer

FDA does not issue a certificate of registration, and it does not recognize one issued by a private business. Registering a food facility with FDA is free ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/guidance-regulation-food-and-dietary-supplements/questions-regarding-whether-food-facilities-are-required-pay-registration-fees-and-private)). A company selling an "FDA certificate" is selling a registration service; FDA says such businesses are not affiliated with it and do not act on its behalf.

What FDA does check depends on what you ship and who imports it:

| What | Who it applies to | What FDA expects | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Food facility registration | Every plant that makes, packs or holds food for the US | Registration before work for the US starts; renewal from October 1 to December 31 of every even-numbered year | [21 CFR 1.230](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-1.230) |
| Foreign Supplier Verification Program (FSVP) | The US importer | A written program that verifies each foreign supplier | [21 CFR 1.502](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-1.502) |
| Canning registration and process filing | Plants that make acidified or low-acid canned foods | Registration and a filed process for each product | [21 CFR 108.25](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-108.25) |
| Food-contact authorization | Packaging makers and their material suppliers | An FDA authorization for each substance in the material | [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/packaging-food-contact-substances-fcs/determining-regulatory-status-components-food-contact-material) |

## Registration: before the first shipment, renewed this quarter

A facility must register before it begins to manufacture, process, pack or hold food for consumption in the US ([21 CFR 1.230(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-1.230)). A foreign facility names a US agent, with an address, phone number and email, and every registration carries a unique facility identifier that FDA accepts ([21 CFR 1.232](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-1.232)).

Registrations are renewed from October 1 to December 31 of each even-numbered year ([21 CFR 1.230(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-1.230)). The current window closes on December 31, 2026.

## FSVP: your US importer's job, and FDA enforces it

Under FSVP, the importer is the US owner or consignee of the food when it is offered for import. If there is none, it is the US agent or representative of the foreign owner, and only if that person signs a statement consenting to serve as importer ([21 CFR 1.500](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-1.500)). The importer must have a written program ([21 CFR 1.502](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-1.502)) and must verify its suppliers through onsite audits, sampling and testing, a review of the supplier's food safety records, or other suitable activities ([21 CFR 1.506](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-1.506)). Much of that evidence comes from you, the supplier.

FSVP is where FDA finds the most problems in food. In fiscal year 2025, FDA inspectors cited importers 483 times for having no FSVP, the most-cited observation in food inspections that year ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/media/190190/download?attachment)).

On April 29, 2026, FDA sent a warning letter to a Philadelphia importer of phyllo dough, Turkish delight and baklava: it had not developed, maintained and followed an FSVP for them. FDA asked for a written reply within 15 working days. The supplier's name and country are redacted ([FDA warning letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/sahara-enterprise-inc-728927-04292026)).

An importer that does not comply can be placed on Import Alert 99-41, and FDA can then detain its food without examining it. To come off the list, the importer, not the foreign supplier, must show that it has fixed the problem ([Import Alert 99-41](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cms_ia/importalert_1160.html)). For the supplier abroad, that means a US customer whose shipments wait until its program is in order.

## Pickled, jarred and canned foods need two filings

Acidified foods are low-acid foods with added acid, such as peppers, cucumbers, cabbage and artichokes, with a finished pH of 4.6 or below; they may be sold as pickles ([21 CFR 114.3](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-114.3)). A plant that makes them, or low-acid canned foods, registers with FDA and files the process for each product ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/acidified-low-acid-canned-foods-lacf-registration/establishment-registration-and-process-filing-acidified-and-low-acid-canned-foods-lacf-electronic)). For acidified foods, a plant abroad registers before it offers them for import, and files each process within 60 days of registering and before packing a new product ([21 CFR 108.25](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-108.25)).

The process must be set by a qualified person with expert knowledge of acidifying and processing these foods, usually called a process authority ([21 CFR 114.83](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-114.83)). FDA can detain acidified and low-acid canned foods that arrive without a filed process ([Import Alert 99-37](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cms_ia/importalert_1131.html)).

## Packaging: there is no FDA approval to buy

Each substance in a food-contact material has to be covered by an authorization: a regulation in Title 21, GRAS status, a prior sanction, a threshold of regulation exemption or an effective food contact notification. A food contact notification belongs to the manufacturer it was filed for, so the substance must be bought from that manufacturer. FDA puts the duty to make sure materials comply on the manufacturer of the food contact substance, and a customer may ask a manufacturer for a letter of guaranty that a product is acceptable for its intended food-contact use ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/packaging-food-contact-substances-fcs/determining-regulatory-status-components-food-contact-material)).

## Certifications are a separate question

SQF, BRCGS, IFS, organic, kosher and halal certificates come from certification bodies, not from FDA. A US buyer may ask for one, but none of them replaces registration, FSVP or a filed process.

## If you own your US importer

A US company in which one foreign owner holds 25% or more files Form 5472 for each year it has transactions with that owner. Each missing form costs [$25,000](/en/insights/form-5472-penalty).

## What to do now

1. Renew your food facility registration by December 31, 2026, and ignore offers of an "FDA certificate".
2. Ask your US importer who its FSVP importer is and which of your records its program needs.
3. If you ship pickled, jarred or canned foods, confirm that every product has a filed process before the next shipment.
4. If you make packaging, list each material's authorization before a buyer asks for it.
